How it works
Apply sends Set, <name>, <value> on the serial link. Any incoming line of the form Gain: 16x, Gain = 16, Set, Gain, 16x, Report, Gain, 16x — or a CSV row under a header carrying that column — updates “Latest reported”, and the status tag says whether the device confirmed the value you chose.

Read me

This page reads a sensor over USB and saves the numbers as a .csv file you can open in Excel. Set up for the lab's AS7341 spectrophotometer. It measures how much light gets through the cuvette in several colour bands (channels).

1. Choose what you're measuring

Settings › What are you measuring? The choice is shown at the top of the page.

  • OD600 (default): cell growth. Uses the 580–600 nm channel, the closest the AS7341 has to 600 nm.
  • DNS assay: reducing sugars at 515 nm. Uses the 505–525 nm channel.
  • Any serial device: any other sensor. You pick the value to log with + Variable of interest.

2. Connect

  1. Plug in the spectrophotometer and click the USB button (top right). Pick the port the first time.
  2. The big number on Reading starts to change. It's the light reaching the sensor: more light = bigger number.
  3. If the number sits at its maximum and doesn't change, lower the Gain (Settings). If it's very small, raise it. Use the same gain for the blank and all samples.

3. Measure: blank first, then samples

  1. Put the blank in (medium or water with reagents, no cells or sugar). Wait until the number is steady.
  2. Type a label (e.g. Blank) under Saved data points and press Save. The first Save asks where to put the file; every Save after that is written straight away.
  3. Do the same for each standard and sample, with a clear label (e.g. Std 0.5 g/L, Culture 2 h).

Absorbance (or OD) is worked out from the blank reading I0 and the sample reading I:

A = log10( I0 / I )

A sample that lets through half as much light as the blank has A = 0.30.

4. Record a time series (optional)

The record button (●, top right) writes every reading to a .csv every 2 s until you press it again, which is useful to watch a culture or reaction over time. On Android the file downloads when you stop.

5. The other tabs

  • Trend: a live plot since you connected. Once a blank is saved it shows absorbance / OD, with your saved points marked. The Steady tag (also on Reading) turns green when the last 8 s stay within 1 %: that's the moment to press Save.
  • Analysis works on your saved points:
    • Calibration curve (DNS assay): type each standard's concentration. The page fits C = k × A through the origin (the same as Excel's LINEST(C, A, FALSE)), shows k and R², and gives every sample's concentration (× its dilution).
    • Growth curve (OD600): OD × dilution against time, with the growth rate μ and the doubling time. Untick the lag or stationary points to leave them out of the fit.
    • Download table gives the whole table and the fit, ready for your report.
    • Time series from a .csv file keeps the older tools: any column against any other, summary statistics, a linear fit, a moving average.
  • Settings: what you're measuring, automatic channel, and the gain.
  • Console: the raw lines from the device, and a box to send commands.

Computers, Ubuntu and Android

  • Use Chrome or Edge. Firefox and Safari can't talk to USB devices.
  • Ubuntu: if the port won't open, the message under the USB button gives the one-off fix (a udev rule, or the dialout group).
  • Android: Chrome with a USB-C OTG cable. Works with native-USB boards (ESP32-S3/C3/C6 and similar). Recordings download when you stop; a copy stays under Settings.

Something wrong?

  • The port won't open: close other tabs, the Arduino serial monitor or any other program using it, then try again.
  • No number appears: open Console to check lines arrive, and check Settings shows the right measurement.
  • Saved points didn't go to a file: use Download next to the saved points.
